Ray of Hope

Ray of Hope 4.0 — inspiring dreams for a colourful future

The fourth edition of our flagship outreach closed the way every edition does: with young people standing beside work they had made themselves.

Ray of Hope · Ibadan, Oyo State · 4th edition

Ray of Hope is the programme everything else grew out of. It is a multi-week outreach delivered inside a single underprivileged community — part encouragement, part practical training — and it has now run four times.

What an edition actually looks like

The format has stayed consistent because it works. We go into a community and stay there for several weeks rather than visiting once. Volunteers and role models run mentoring sessions alongside hands-on training. Children and young adults learn something they can earn from — crochet, fabric work, craft — and they learn it slowly enough to actually keep the skill.

The point is not a photograph of a donation handover. The point is that at the end of the weeks, a young person can make something, show it to a room, and be paid for it.

The closing day

Every edition ends with an exhibition, trade fair and awards ceremony. Trainees present what they have produced. Community members, parents and guests are invited. Items are displayed and sold. Awards recognise participation and effort.

For many participants this is the first time anyone has paid them for something they made with their own hands. It is a small amount of money and a very large change in self-perception.

You do not lift a child out of poverty with one gift. You do it by handing them something they can repeat.

Chrysolite Foundation

Where the programme goes next

Some children who come through Ray of Hope have a more urgent problem than skills — a school fee, a uniform, a set of books. Those cases are referred into Back to School, which is now in its third edition.

And the things trainees make do not stop at the exhibition table. They are listed in our shop, where 100% of sales contribute to future trainings. That is how an edition pays for the next one.

How to be part of the next edition

  • Give. Donations fund materials, venue and logistics — see ways to donate.
  • Volunteer. Facilitators, mentors, photographers and logistics hands are all needed — see volunteering.
  • Buy. Purchasing what trainees make keeps the loop running — visit the shop.
