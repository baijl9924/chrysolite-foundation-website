Shop with purpose

Everything here was made by a young person in our training programme. 100% of these sales contribute to future trainings.

The loop

How buying keeps the programme running

Most charity shops ask you to donate and then separately ask you to buy. Ours is simpler and, we think, stronger: our trainees learn a craft, make real products, and those products are sold. The trainee earns from their own work — and the proceeds are turned straight back into training the next group.

That means a purchase from this page does two things at once. It pays for something handmade, and it funds the next six-week cohort.

01

We train

Children and young adults join a six-week skill empowerment cohort in their own community.

02

They make

Crochet sling bags, scrunchies, adire fabric and handmade ankara books — produced by hand.

03

You buy

Items are listed in our online store. The trainee earns from the work of their own hands.

04

It repeats

100% of these sales contribute to future trainings — the next cohort is funded by the last one.

Made by our trainees

What they make

Availability changes with each cohort — the store is the live list. Below is what our trainees have produced so far.

Crochet sling bags

Hand-crocheted, one at a time, in the colours each trainee chooses. No two are identical.

Scrunchies

A first project that teaches tension and finishing — and sells. Small, useful, handmade.

Adire fabric

Traditional Yoruba resist-dyed cloth, made by our trainees using adire techniques.

Ankara books

Notebooks bound in ankara fabric — where the skill programme and school materials meet.

For organisations

Buy in bulk, or commission a batch

If you are buying for a team, an event, a school or a church group, we can take a bulk order. It is a genuinely useful form of support: it gives our trainees a real production deadline and a real customer.

  • Branded or custom-colour items, subject to lead time
  • Conference and event gifts
  • Resale arrangements for shops and online sellers
  • A written note on how the order funded training
